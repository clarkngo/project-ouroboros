# Chapter 16 — The Circuit Breaker

**Synopsis:** Devon leads the first real prototype of a company-wide circuit-breaker framework — generalizing his Ch. 1 step-limit patch and Ch. 10's least-privilege scoping into reusable infrastructure rather than one-off fixes. Internal pushback from other teams who experience the new hard limits as a velocity tax, echoing the objections Claire raised (and outgrew) earlier in the book — a deliberate structural rhyme, now voiced by new, more minor characters so Claire doesn't have to relitigate a position she's already moved past.

**Key beats:**
- Devon, now partway through his own arc, becomes an unexpected advocate for the discipline he used to route around — a visible sign of his Ch. 12 lapse having actually changed him rather than just being narratively punished.
- Technical set piece: the team defines the specific taxonomy of limits (step count, cost ceiling, tool-call rate, wall-clock time) as a shared framework other teams can adopt without each reinventing Devon's original 4 a.m. hack.
- Sloan's schema-validation work (seeded in Ch. 10) and Devon's circuit breakers begin to visibly converge into one coherent architecture — the chapter should make the reader feel the Act III harness assembling itself from parts introduced separately across the book.
- Ends on cautious optimism — deliberately, to make Ch. 17's outage land harder by contrast.
