# Chapter 18 — The Reckoning

**Synopsis:** The aftermath of Ch. 17 and the true hinge point of the book. The company faces a real inflection: retreat to a more conservative, less autonomous product (Claire's board pushing this direction, framed as the "safe" choice) or commit fully, organization-wide, to finishing the deterministic-scaffolding architecture that's been built in pieces throughout Act II. Closes Act II.

**Key beats:**
- Board pressure resurfaces (callback to Ch. 2 and Ch. 11) but inverted in substance: this time the conservative-sounding option (pull back autonomy broadly) is shown to be the less rigorous one — a panic response rather than an engineering one — while the harder, less intuitive choice (finish the harness properly) is the disciplined one. Victor likely articulates this distinction explicitly.
- Alex, Claire, Devon, and Sloan aligned in the same room for the first time as genuine peers rather than adversaries or supplicants — the chapter's emotional payoff after two acts of friction between exactly these four.
- Claire makes the case to the board herself this time, unprompted, using the vocabulary (bounded tails, evals as evidence, scoped autonomy) she's absorbed across the book — a clear marker of her arc's completion, without requiring her to become an engineer.
- Ends on explicit institutional commitment to what Act III will build in earnest, with real stakes attached: the financial-services account's survival is made contingent on delivering it, giving Act III a ticking clock.
