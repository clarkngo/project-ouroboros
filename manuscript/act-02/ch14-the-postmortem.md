# Chapter 14 — The Postmortem

**Synopsis:** A blameless postmortem that isn't quite blameless in practice — the chapter's tension is the gap between Meridian's stated engineering culture (blameless, systems-focused) and the real political fallout when a major account is shaken. Devon's bypassed eval run from Ch. 12 comes out in the room. Alex has to decide, in real time, how to hold Devon accountable to the discipline she asked him to build without recreating the SPOF dynamic by shielding him, and without scapegoating him for a pressure (Ch. 11's compressed timeline) that was imposed from above him.

**Key beats:**
- Direct confrontation between Alex and Claire about whose pressure produced the conditions for the lapse — the Ch. 2 dynamic fully inverted: Alex is now the one saying "I told you what we needed and you compressed the timeline anyway."
- Devon owns the omission without being allowed to martyr himself over it either — Victor or Alex reframes it for him in systems terms: a process with no enforcement mechanism relies on willpower under pressure, and willpower is not a control system. The fix isn't "try harder," it's "make the gate impossible to route around."
- First explicit articulation, by Alex, of what becomes the Act III thesis: the org needs *deterministic* gates, not well-intentioned humans doing the right thing under pressure every single time.
- Ends with real, unresolved friction between Alex and Claire — not fixed by one good meeting — setting up Ch. 15's turning point with Victor as the chapter that actually starts to repair it.
