# Chapter 21 — Sloan's Gate

**Synopsis:** Sloan's culminating chapter — the security review process that was, across Acts I and II, a slow, ad hoc, case-by-case negotiation (Ch. 5, Ch. 10, Ch. 13) becomes a fast, staged, systematic gate that every tool integration and prompt change passes through automatically, using the schema validation and eval infrastructure built in Ch. 19–20. Direct payoff of the book's thesis: the same harness that gives Claire a defensible SLA gives Sloan a scalable, non-bottlenecked security practice.

**Key beats:**
- A new tool integration request (deliberately similar in shape to Ch. 5's Vantage Rail ask, as a structural callback) moves from request to scoped, tested, approved deployment in days rather than six-to-eight weeks — shown concretely, not just asserted.
- Sloan gets a moment of rare, explicit vindication — someone (possibly a platform engineer who was dismissive of her in Ch. 10) directly acknowledges she was right, and Sloan's characteristically flat, ungloating response is itself characterful.
- Establishes the standing adversarial-eval regression practice as permanent infrastructure, not a one-time hardening exercise — directly resolving the "unmeasured risk" framing established as her core objection since Ch. 5.
- Sets up Ch. 22's relaunch by clearing the last structural obstacle to it.
